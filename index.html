The model

How the program works

A simple operating rhythm the Integration Chair can repeat each year without rebuilding the program from scratch.

1

Source Navigators

Use two years of chapter attendance data to identify active, experienced members who are already showing up.

2

Recruit + Match

Invite the strongest candidates personally, then pair them with incoming members based on likely fit.

3

Track 30 / 60 / 90

Monitor the first meeting, shared events, warm introductions and overall integration progress.

4

Reconnect Mid-Year

Bring Navigators and new members together for a dinner focused on deeper EO value and new connections.

Repeatable process

Navigator sourcing + annual operating rhythm

Attendance creates the objective shortlist. Judgment determines who should represent EO Toronto to a new member.

2 yrs

Start with actual engagement

Ask Carolina for the previous two full years of event attendance, then rank members by total participation before applying the human filter.

Start of board yearBuild the pool
  1. Ask Carolina for member event attendance from the previous two full chapter years.
  2. Rank members by total events attended across both years.
  3. Remove non-renewed or inactive members and flag current board members.
  4. Review top members for suitability: 2+ years in EO, reliable, welcoming, responsive and good at making introductions.
  5. Create Priority A, B and backup Navigator candidates.
  6. Send the Navigator outreach note with the one-page cheat sheet.
  7. Confirm the active Navigator pool and how many new members each person can support.
As members joinMatch + integrate
  1. Match the new member with an available Navigator based on likely fit, interests, experience and geography.
  2. Send a joint introduction.
  3. Track the first meeting, event attendance, introductions and 30/60/90 status.
Mid-yearReconnect
  1. Host a Navigator + New Member dinner.
  2. Use the dinner to uncover untapped EO opportunities, useful connections and members who may be drifting.
Year-end handoffMake it sustainable
  1. Thank and recognize Navigators.
  2. Review what worked and retain strong Navigators for the following year.
  3. Hand off the attendance data, prospect list, outreach templates, cheat sheet and tracker to the next Integration Chair.

Recruitment approach

Make it feel like recognition, not volunteering.

Prospective Navigators should know they are being approached because they are active, trusted members who can help a new member feel connected quickly.

Copy-paste template Personal invitation · cheat sheet attached
Subject: Would you be an EO Toronto Navigator? Hi [Name], I’m putting together this year’s EO Toronto Navigator group and wanted to ask if you’d be willing to participate. The Navigator Program pairs newer EO members with experienced members who can help them get connected to the chapter, meet the right people, understand the opportunities available to them and generally get more value from EO sooner. I’m reaching out to you specifically because you’ve been one of the more engaged members of the chapter, and I think you’d be a great person for a new member to have in their corner during their first year. The commitment is deliberately pretty light: • one coffee or meaningful conversation early on • attend a couple of EO events together • make a few thoughtful introductions • stay loosely connected through their first year I’ve attached a one-page Navigator cheat sheet that explains the role and expectations. The goal isn’t for you to mentor them or become responsible for their EO experience. It’s really just to be a familiar face, a connector and someone who can help them find their way into the chapter. Would you be open to being part of the Navigator group this year? Thanks — I’d really appreciate it. Lee
